\# 08. Executive Risk Briefing



\## Executive Summary

Riverside Family Health Partners faces a realistic ransomware and phishing risk profile typical of a small healthcare provider. The highest exposures are incomplete MFA coverage, backup recovery uncertainty, limited logging, and inconsistent operational preparedness.



\## Key Findings

1\. Email account compromise is a credible path to broader system compromise.

2\. Ransomware could significantly disrupt patient care and billing operations.

3\. Backup availability does not guarantee backup recoverability.

4\. Current controls appear foundational but not yet mature or consistently managed.

5\. Incident response and continuity planning need clearer ownership and testing.



\## Top Risks

\- account compromise through phishing

\- ransomware affecting EHR and file access

\- data loss or delayed recovery due to backup weaknesses

\- weak access control and review processes

\- vendor-related exposure



\## Business Impact

A major cyber incident could result in:

\- inability to access patient records

\- appointment and telehealth disruption

\- billing delays

\- compliance concerns

\- reputational damage

\- overtime and recovery costs



\## Recommended Leadership Actions

\- fund and prioritize MFA enforcement

\- require restore testing for backups

\- approve incident response roles and procedures

\- formalize a 90-day remediation plan

\- assign accountable owners for high-risk items



\## Management Decision Needed

Leadership should decide:

\- risk appetite for delayed remediation

\- acceptable recovery objectives

\- budget for security tooling and training

\- ownership model for ongoing security governance
